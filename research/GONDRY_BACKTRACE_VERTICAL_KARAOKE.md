# The Gondry move: solve the motion BACKWARDS, run Conway FORWARDS

**A B3/S23 cellular automaton is not reversible in general.** In particular,
one cannot reverse arbitrary glider collisions or restore one-shot catalysts
just by running their images in reverse. But the free-flight orbit of an
isolated period-four lightweight spaceship (LWSS) is known exactly:

- A real westbound nine-cell LWSS advances **(-2,0) every four generations**.
- Rotate that native Life pattern 90 degrees: it advances **(0,+2) every four generations**.
- Demand a word pixel at screen coordinates (x,y) on Suno generation G,
  where G is a multiple of four.
- Preload the exact same southbound LWSS at generation zero at **(x,y-G/2)**.

This is the physical equivalent of Gondry's reverse-filmed choreography:
we stage the required final action first and calculate the initial positions
backwards. **The exported universe is then evolved normally under B3/S23.**
There are no reversed video frames or per-frame Life-cell injections.

## What this replaces

The earlier karaoke tried to create leftbound LWSSes *during* playback
using down-moving spaceships and one-shot turners, and later a reusable
2023 glider-to-LWSS converter. That infrastructure is unnecessary if the
artistic requirement is "words approaching from ABOVE and passing through
the singer's reading area." We can initialize all moving spaceships upstream
at g0, far above the future reading plane, without trying to manufacture
thousands of them during the video.

The full source is generated by `life/gondry_vertical_karaoke.py` from
the exact 403 original Suno word onsets at **192 generations/second**.
The physically smaller 3x4 font uses **10 Life cells per horizontal pixel
and 8 per vertical pixel**, each one a genuine nine/twelve-cell southbound
LWSS. There are **10,366 independent glyph-pixel ships**, all present
at g0; no glyph pixel is generated by the compositor.

## Whole-song spatial constraints and reusable cleanup

Some original Suno attacks are separated by only 48 generations. A full
four-row glyph is about 29 native cells tall including the moving ships.
At c/2 that would overlap in a single vertical text lane. Therefore
we allocate successive words to parallel **physical horizontal lanes**
until their same-lane start times are at least 64 generations apart.
This is genuine B3/S23 geometry, not a visual substitution. The leftmost
cell of each word crosses its chosen fixed horizontal reading plane
at the exact original Suno-aligned generation. Multiple words can be
in-flight, as in a genuine conveyor.

The native south-facing seven-cell fishhook is a D4 rotation of the
existing qualified EATER_LEFT. Every column has a persistent fishhook
**160 native cells south** of the reading plane. A single incoming ship
does not disturb the receiver until local generation 37, and by
generation 40 the exact original seven-cell eater is back with zero
ash. Each same-column pair of sources is separated by >=8 native
vertical cells (>=16-generation arrival gap). No two eating reactions
overlap, and the west-to-south rotated collision remains physically
the *same* B3/S23 reaction.

Horizontal column pitch 10 is intentionally wider than the eight-cell
total reaction envelope [-3,+4], ensuring disjoint Moore neighborhoods
between different columns, including all receiver transients. The
four-step southbound phases are certified individually. This yields a
composable exact-evolution argument for an arbitrarily long finite
pulse train *using this specific emitter phase and spacing*. An actual
whole-world B3/S23 forward run of the original first two lyric words
WE (g2156) and MADE (g2204), including source ablation, checks
the independent formulation. A separate C++ Life engine also checks
all 326 original full-world 8-generation snapshots through g2600.

The last sung word begins at g44768. The physically moving letter
ships arrive at their receiver banks and disappear before the music
ends at g45864. The **stationary receiver banks persist**; we have
NOT implemented the user-requested all-zero apocalypse.
