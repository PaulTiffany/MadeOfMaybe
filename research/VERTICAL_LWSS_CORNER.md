# Real 90-degree moving-letter corner: from above into the lyric rail

The proposed geometry is feasible. The project now includes a minimal
**native B3/S23 one-shot proof** based on knightlife's [2009 LWSS
turner](https://conwaylife.com/forums/viewtopic.php?p=633). With one
orientation of the reaction, a 9-cell LWSS arriving **vertically from
above** at c/2 enters a 19-cell beehive/boat/pond station. By generation
200, the collision has produced an isolated 12-cell phase LWSS leaving
**horizontally to the left** at c/2. Another 40 generations of exact native
Life evolution verifies its clean leftward velocity. An input-ablated
station produces no outbound spaceship.

Three tests qualify the input, exact native output, and Golly RLE.

**This does NOT qualify a repeating corner.** The 19-cell catalyst set
is not restored after the reaction; additional emitted gliders/debris
must be handled. A whole 5x7 font needs up to seven separately positioned
output rows, adequate separation between successive LWSS pixels, and
real rearming/clean routing. A *single* small one-shot device cannot
instantly rotate an entire convoy or pass hundreds of pixels intact.
A stable indirect alternative is LWSS-to-glider, glider reflectors,
then glider-to-LWSS; known converters have nonzero footprints and
recovery times. These are engineering inputs, not a finished compiler.

In the intended scene, incoming letter-forming ships descend on several
parallel tracks ABOVE the singer's speech bubble; at the corner, native
turning/conversion stations emit westbound LWSS trains. The final
horizontal passage through the bubble is the reading line, followed
by the already qualified row eaters to the left. Word spacing and
phrase blank intervals are actual separation in the stream. Physically
driven highlighting/mouth/phrase-symbol signals must attach to this
routing after the repeated corner is independently qualified.
