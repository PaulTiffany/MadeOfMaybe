# Physically reduced lyric + full 238.875s source staging — qualification

## A physically smaller moving word, not a camera trick

The user wants the LWSS karaoke roughly half the relative size of
the singer's mouth. Ordinary Conway LWSS patterns **cannot be scaled**
like raster graphics. We changed the glyph itself from 5×7 to a
more compact **3×4** Life-ship font. The previously qualified one-shot
19-cell vertical→left LWSS turners keep their 36-cell spacing and
consequently retain actual native traffic clearance.

The initial full singer universe shrinks from **8,867 to 8,090**
live cells; the 35 visible WE LWSS pixels become **17**. The nominal
WE envelope shrinks from 396×252 to **252×144 Life cells** (36%
narrower, 43% shorter). At **g2156** (original Suno word onset)
all **17 actual LWSS** pixels occupy the intended mini glyph positions
and, by g2180, have moved exactly 12 cells left. The genuine
word-LWSS → companion marker → independent Suno vocal W/V glider
collision and lip articulation are preserved intact.

We checked the exact combined B3/S23 initial world (8,090 cells)
against five independent components at **every generation g0–3840**,
with **no unplanned cross-component collisions**, and rendered
**481 uninterrupted 24fps frames** through the same native-camera
renderer, with the owner's original audio muxed afterward. The
combined world has exactly **7,372 cells at g3840**. There are no
editor-painted lyrics or mouth expressions, frames, borders, or cuts.

The 3×4 samples are intentionally a coarse font; some letters may
lose distinctive strokes. This is a legitimate physical trade-off
and not proof that any 403-word text can be conveyed at this pitch.

## Entire original song's independent temporal inputs are now compiled

The source tape loads **403 word-onset gliders** directly from the
original Suno alignment and **106 vocal-boundary gliders** (53 starts,
53 ends) from the independently measured isolated Suno vocal stem.
Every glider is a genuine five-cell southeast-moving Life pattern,
present at g0, following a mathematically separated c/4 rail. W uses
4 lanes and V uses 3, with at least 64 generations between gliders
sharing a lane. Entire-song timestamps are 192 B3/S23 generations per
audio second, covering **45864 generations** (~238.875s).

A finite-speed invariant proves that these deliberately remote
prototype input rails **cannot interfere** with the singer's native
first-20s scene during the music. This does not qualify downstream
decision-circuit behavior: the tape is **physically unwired** to the
mouth and currently does NOT make the 403 word glyphs appear.

## Critical unfinished function

The original 19-cell spaceship right-angle turner is **one-shot**.
Even with only ~half as many pixels per glyph, the whole song's
403 source words require 10,366 moving mini-glyph pixel events.
A controller capable of handling that traffic in ONE shared rounded
bubble, erasing each word, satisfying physical recovery/throughput
constraints, and maintaining source-gated medium/open held notes
has NOT been built or certified.

Experiments with naïvely extending the original glider-lip score to
all 332 target changes generated g0 source overlaps and corrupted
later mouth poses; simply stamping thousands of successful isolated
glider recipes into the same universe is **not** sufficient.
Do not use video-renderer mouth overrides, word sprites, staged
camera pans or artificially reinitialized life worlds to hide
that unresolved native scheduling/turner-reuse issue.

**This sprint delivers an honest smaller native first-20s video and
a complete but unconnected Suno W/V input program. It is NOT
the finished 3:58.88 native Conway karaoke film.**
